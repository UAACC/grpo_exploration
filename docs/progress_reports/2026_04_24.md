## 3. what's actually happening inside dg's gate

i started wondering about something this week. our teacher (qwen2.5-math-7b-instruct) and student (qwen2.5-0.5b-instruct) are from the same model family. same tokenizer, same chat template, same general instruction-tuning style. so a typical teacher completion probably doesn't look very surprising to the student. but dg's whole "four-quadrant noise filter" picture relies on surprisal being a strong signal that varies across rollouts. if surprisal is uniformly low, the sigmoid gate is stuck in its linear regime and dg basically degenerates into a half-weight reinforce on the advantage. that would change how we explain dg's role in this paper.

so i decided to actually measure it. wrote a quick diagnostic: for each of the 4 datasets, load ~2000 teacher completions, compute mean per-token surprisal under the base student (the same formula dg uses at training time), group-normalize advantages, compute delight = advantage × surprisal, then the gate at several η. ran in 3 minutes on one l40s.

### surprisal split by correctness (mean per-token, nats)

| dataset | correct | wrong | wrong/correct ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| math | 0.190 | 0.987 | 5.2× |
| gsm8k | 0.173 | 1.477 | 8.5× |
| svamp | 0.224 | 4.465 | **20×** |
| asdiv | 0.211 | 1.278 | 6.1× |

correct teacher completions are uniformly low-surprisal (~0.2 nats), so they all live in the linear regime of the sigmoid regardless of η. wrong completions are heavy-tailed and 5 to 20x more surprising on average.

### gate saturation at η=0.1 (most aggressive)

| dataset | frac<0.1 (filtered out) | frac>0.9 (amplified) |
|---|---|---|
| math | 3.8% | 1.9% |
| gsm8k | 1.4% | 0.8% |
| svamp | 3.6% | 2.5% |
| asdiv | 1.4% | 0.9% |

even at the most aggressive η, only 2 to 6% of samples saturate the gate. the rest sit in the linear regime where dg ≈ a half-weight reweighting of reinforce.

### what this means

1. the four-quadrant "noise filter" picture is real but narrow. dg's gate catches the truly garbled 2 to 6% tail of wrong teacher completions (truncated, repetitive, weird), not the bulk of wrong-but-normal-looking completions.
2. the "amplify surprising successes" branch barely fires because teacher correctness looks like student-default behavior. positive delights are tiny.
3. η is mostly working as a sigmoid gain, not a filter sharpness. low η pushes the sparse high-|delight| tail into saturation, which is why gsm8k and svamp both pick η=0.1.
4. the dataset-dependence of η has a candidate mechanistic story: each dataset's wrong-completion surprisal distribution has a different std, so each needs a different η to put the tail at the saturation boundary. asdiv's optimum at η=2.0 is consistent with this (asdiv's wrong tail isn't as extreme as svamp's, so a softer gate is fine).

per-completion records (qid, reward, surprisal, advantage, delight) are saved for follow-up plots if we want to put a histogram in the paper.
